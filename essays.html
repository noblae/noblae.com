Physiospiritual

The pre civilized religions were stronger because they had still retained the duality of physicality and spirituality – what is really the same thing but language has managed to somehow split apart like the mind and the body which is simply the nervous system, this too has its own extrasensory energy –what I term physiospiritual, essentially the extrasensory unconscious most often associated with dreams and visions and the physical unconscious associated with muscle memory and instinct. I put these together because in the case of an individual life, the sensation or a very strong feeling pulling one in a certain direction is divine because we are fated to live our life in a certain way: the further we are pulled from this emotional center of what is most natural and beautiful in us, the more miserable and discontent we feel; the closer, the more free and liberated we feel. We do not choose to be who we really are. In fact, we must obey the truth of what we have been born into in the sense of our biology (the traits and genetics we receive from our parents, the son and daughter are the reflection of the strongest instincts and deepest held dreams and passions of their parents ), our historical period (it is impossible to be born in another time: we have to accept it), our place in the world (race, gender, nationality) and our bodies (height, physical ability). For better or for worse. Across the history of human kind from its dawn to its current period human beings have followed this collective unconscious made clear in their nervous system and interests to create the current existence that we occupy, and this started with archaic and primitive man, without language or rationality. And it is still this which we work from today because consciousness is constructed upon the foundation of something reptilian, irrational, preverbal. They were acting from their bodies and something extrasensory, not fully understood, but heard loud and clear: the messages given within the body causing the slight change in the tension of muscle, the rapidity of breath at the sight of certain people, the sharpness of vision or sudden abrupt stop in the process of digestion for some unknown danger, all of this is one and the same with the seemingly ‘opposite’ world of the spiritual unconscious, and in fact these physiological sensations may even be able to be viewed as a physical ‘placement’ of the individual’s destiny and the entire destiny of humankind into their body for the purpose of creating the necessary actions from their past due to its ebb and flow of emotions, the cause and effect of their fated birth leading to the destiny of their highest good and the highest good of all mankind. And when we look around at our surroundings with the understanding that all this came from the foundation of animals who could hardly communicate and lived in constant chaos, it is hard not to believe that the universe has a direction and meaning for individuals and the entirety of humanity. The architect of the universe did not build a stairway leading nowhere

religion, law, and capitalism

religion did not arise out of a need for a union with the divine or an experience of the divine but from a need to impose a good or bad, a vision of a hell and a vision of a heaven and the associated punishments and rewards to those who follow each. Violence and the constant threat of violence reinforced these social rules which had become so charged that they took on religious and metaphysical weight due to their importance in the improvement of the human species. The etymology of the word law reveals this: religious law and common law were indistinguishable, religion and its attempts to create a higher type of person necessitated tightened restrictions, tightened punishments, tightened discipline to create people like diamonds from the external pressure. And like the blobfish who is accustomed to living under extreme pressure at extreme depths, the human being who has now been exposed to the ridiculously liberated and unrestricted and relaxed morality generated by free market capitalism soon becomes uglier and more repulsive in its aesthetic and moral vacuum

Sport and religion and pankration

The emergence and revitalization of physical culture in the late eighteen hundreds and early nineteen hundreds was due to the decline in Christianity observed and predicted by men like Nietzsche and the re-integration of sport as a vital part of human life, as a higher morality towards which human beings have some goal, some aim, something towards which they can actually strive, an image of perfection, an icon: in our time: the Christian ascetic replaced by the tragic athlete. The return of physical culture is the return of Roman values. It is no mistake that the first truly Christian emperor who condemned humanity to a sedentary existence was also – the one who banned the Olympics, the one who canceled this pagan celebration. And how much pressure had been built over two millennia, how much energy was released within such a short amount of time!

Following the death of God, humanity was able to re-experience some of the splendor known by the Greeks and Romans in their celebrations, a rare feat in a pacing, screaming and adrenaline fueled society: the recreation of Olympic Games in 1896, which contained the ‘new’ sports of freestyle and Greco-Roman wrestling; the early, primitive form of bodybuilding in the late eighteen hundreds into the early nineteen hundreds, the offspring of circus shows and strongman competitions, and now into the twenty first century the re-emergence of the height of Roman sport – pankration/MMA – is the sign of the complete death of the values promoted by Christianity. The only difference between this period and the previous Roman version of physical culture is that our culture has filtered the human body and the spectacle of athletic competition through the lens of capitalism whereas the Romans viewed this as a religious event, an intoxicating, almost artistic event. The sheer volume of statues and bas reliefs and vases created during this time depicting Roman and Greek athletes in the heat of competition shows the importance with which these cultures treated sport. The entire center of their energy was built upon something highly dramatic, violent, and almost irrational, and the poetry and plays and sports they engaged in was a reflection of this dramatic and irrational but incredibly strong disposition one that is too often forgotten in the modern age which has become thoroughly weak from rationality and sedentary living: the rage of Achilles, the obsession of Oedipus.

A short historical lesson: the Olympics in antiquity was an ode and performance dedicated to human vitality and strength, they were an event dedicated to Zeus, the king of their gods. Animals were sacrificed to the gods on Olympus, and here perhaps as a psychological observation it can be seen that the deeper one treads into sensory and visceral and instinctual existence the more deep they tread into the unknown, the preverbal and symbolic, the unconscious. The Christian starved ascetic stabbed the Roman athlete and with him the viscera and blood inherent within human existence died. And what harm, what true harm, as a finalizing actor Theodosius I did as a piece of fate! Like a slaughterhouse worker he had taken this already exhausted and declining civilization and had put the final nail in the coffin, he had castrated and emasculated what was most perfected within this civilization, their religion and their sport. And after two thousand years these events return but without renewed intensity. The modern plastic and white light malaise has already cut and gutted the spirituality from this event.

The distinction between the previous Olympics and the secularized modern Olympics is seen most clearly in how the sport of pankration / mixed martial arts is treated. A reduction to sentences, to simplicity: the absolute height of a religious and athletic event. Two men fighting half naked in a cage for a drunken audience. Mixed martial arts as well as life for modern man ought to be decoupled from the modern associations with corporations and mere entertainment and elevated back to its natural position as the ultimate sport which Greek and Roman poets and philosophers and warriors saw the beautiful nature of humanity, its teeth, its splendor, its lust for life, and the crowning event of this event – what is today called ‘mixed martial arts’ and previously called pankration – should and must be reintegrated into the Olympics: it should be fought in stadiums, amphitheaters, open air – in ancient places where natural light can illumine and create those moments that photography can capture: the clinch, the strikes, blood, sweat, the facial muscles contorting from exertion, the sensory experience and the understanding that this primitive display is the soil from which we were all born, the soil from which the heights of the achievements of civilization: the finest works of visual art, the greatest works of literature, architectural and engineering and design advancements, the discoveries of modern science, the advancement of modern technology, beauty and innovation that is created from the suppression of certain impulses and drives and the elevation of others to be able to achieve something – all of this emerges from the literal, visceral instinctual energy that is expressed throughout this exchange. This sport ought to not only be witnessed as fleeting and momentary scenes of athletic greatness, but as an achievement in and of itself for humankind: these moments of greatness are evidence that man the animal is still capable of inflicting the most primitive and raw violence upon a competitor while, at the exact same time, we as humans still possess the traits which have been cultivated purposefully in man throughout the process of civilization and these athletes display them in the surface and in the depths: discipline, focus, the ability to maintain calm under pressure, courage, the ability to think beyond the present moment and more than that, the ability to be an animal which can decide upon its own fate while acting from a given destiny, all of this against the disorder and chaos and entropy that capitalism throws into the functioning of the nervous system
