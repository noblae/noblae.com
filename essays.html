Physical culture against free market capitalism

Capitalism is an economic system where private property is owned within private hands and this property is used to generate profit, the modern version and interpretation of this economic system seen within the United States is free market capitalism, the liberation of this economic system from the restraint of law. Morality and religion were responses to the condition of human life without restraint and standards, and the rules for what was to be performed and dictated and what was to not be done was previously a religious duty but law had already become completely decoupled from religion and this might have accelerated the decline of Christianity as it liberated mankind from the restrictions which Christianity imposed for so long, until it finally became unbelievable, accelerated by the scientific and physiological discoveries of the 1800s and 1900s – and now religion itself which offered a system of values to attempt to elevate humanity beyond the mere animal, beyond mere life, has been replaced by capitalism which now dictates the values of society towards mere life and acting on immediate, raw impulse and away from delaying gratification. The entire, central goal of civilization – to create the ability to control one’s own behavior – is being undermined through free market capitalism to such a degree that it might be said that it is devolving the human race. The religious foundation of the West has been shattered with a hammer and the entirety of law, religion, conventional Christian morality crumbles down with it, and in the broken glass and shattered remains of civilization the entirety of human existence, thought, not even simply the conscious but the unconscious aspects of all of human life are built upon the back of the naked human body, the body as it is, the body with its own drives separate and distinct from the wishes and demands of society at large, the body which engaged in sacrificial rites, trance and mystical experiences, the body which has its own extrasensory intuition, and this sleeping giant has been reawakened, unchained, and now the body remains the contended upon object which the free market capitalist and the materialist scientist want to vivisect to use this information to turn it into another form of property which it can generate value from, generate money from, whereas the the athlete and philosopher want to elevate it to the level of high art, moving on from the damage, trauma, humiliation and lack of pride, and physical harm inflicted upon the body through Christian mortifications and atonements, the industrial capitalist horrors of machinery and motors and cracked human bones, past the late capitalist which reduces the ingenuity, beauty, grace, strength, and absolute perfection of the human body and its spirit to meat, mere life: a combination of muscle and breath which can be studied and used to understand: a) what makes a human body feel pleasure b) how can we manipulate the systems within the body to exploit this and c) from this understanding, how can we press upon these natural impulses with our fingers to turn blood into money. The most dehumanizing event is occurring with the information age, felt but not fully expressed. A human being reduced to a nervous system with sensory organs which can detect stimuli and experience pleasure. A body which has muscles which can contort and stretch and twist. And here the only real counterpart to this thing which takes the human body and puts it into a meat grinder is the light and dancelike, the ascension of the body into physical and aesthetic beauty through nourishment: the ascension of the spirituality of man from the physical health of his body, the engagement with social and physical activities which impose their own demands, their own set of morals apart from those supported by free market capitalism, the man seen sprinting and exerting himself and laughing and smiling with fellow athletes is the man of this new age and he is in this eternal moment the extension of a thread which has been trying to become born since the dawn of prehistory and through the traumas of the miscarriages and abortions and attempts to crush this human spirit throughout history, the man with a liberated body used for exertion to create beauty and improvement, not punishment, no whip; not mere pleasure, but smiling, is the beautiful man of the modern age that humanity has been waiting to create: smiling and laughing and dancing, dancing in the sun like a child who has reawakened after two thousand years of sleep

Physiospiritual

The pre civilized religions were stronger because they had still retained the duality of physicality and spirituality – what is really the same thing but language has managed to somehow split apart like the mind and the body which is simply the nervous system, this too has its own extrasensory energy –what I term physiospiritual, essentially the extrasensory unconscious most often associated with dreams and visions and the physical unconscious associated with muscle memory and instinct. I put these together because in the case of an individual life, the sensation or a very strong feeling pulling one in a certain direction is divine because we are fated to live our life in a certain way: the further we are pulled from this emotional center of what is most natural and beautiful in us, the more miserable and discontent we feel; the closer, the more free and liberated we feel. We do not choose to be who we really are. In fact, we must obey the truth of what we have been born into in the sense of our biology (the traits and genetics we receive from our parents, the son and daughter are the reflection of the strongest instincts and deepest held dreams and passions of their parents ), our historical period (it is impossible to be born in another time: we have to accept it), our place in the world (race, gender, nationality) and our bodies (height, physical ability). For better or for worse. Across the history of human kind from its dawn to its current period human beings have followed this collective unconscious made clear in their nervous system and interests to create the current existence that we occupy, and this started with archaic and primitive man, without language or rationality. And it is still this which we work from today because consciousness is constructed upon the foundation of something reptilian, irrational, preverbal. They were acting from their bodies and something extrasensory, not fully understood, but heard loud and clear: the messages given within the body causing the slight change in the tension of muscle, the rapidity of breath at the sight of certain people, the sharpness of vision or sudden abrupt stop in the process of digestion for some unknown danger, all of this is one and the same with the seemingly ‘opposite’ world of the spiritual unconscious, and in fact these physiological sensations may even be able to be viewed as a physical ‘placement’ of the individual’s destiny and the entire destiny of humankind into their body for the purpose of creating the necessary actions from their past due to its ebb and flow of emotions, the cause and effect of their fated birth leading to the destiny of their highest good and the highest good of all mankind. And when we look around at our surroundings with the understanding that all this came from the foundation of animals who could hardly communicate and lived in constant chaos, it is hard not to believe that the universe has a direction and meaning for individuals and the entirety of humanity. The architect of the universe did not build a stairway leading nowhere

The height of a religious and athletic event

The human body no longer enters into a religious and athletic event as it did in the early Olympics in Greece and Rome when it competes in a fight, but it enters into a spectacle which is designed to make money and generate a profit. These events were held in the past for the purpose of veneration and adoration of the human body, the celebration of human existence and vitality – the bodies of these athletes were raised to the level of high art with their likeness engraved and carved and painted onto bas reliefs, sculptures, and still images which depicted their moments of athletic greatness. And it was within these men and the art which emerged from them that poets and philosophers saw the beautiful nature of humanity, its teeth, its splendor, its lust for life.

The Olympics, the height of international sport in the modern day, for the Romans and Greeks was not merely an athletic event, but a religious event where wars were stopped, bulls were sacrificed to Zeus, and the greatest athletes of their respective cities were sent to Olympia and at the very height of this physical and spiritual event beneath the sun and light there stood – pankration. A sport which is comparable to modern mixed martial arts, a sport which has now been reduced to mere entertainment, a sport which has been commercialized but still remains charged with the spiritual weight that all athletic events carry. Physicality and spirituality exist in direct proportion to each other, and here it can be seen that the body of the Aztec and his spiritual beliefs, the body of the Roman, the Christian, the capitalist – are inseparable. Their bodies and their spirit are one in the same. The human soul or spirit is a part of the body. It is for this reason that modern life for so many feels emptied out and hollowed. The danger of sedentary living is not only in damaging the body but in damaging the ‘soul’ or ‘spirit,’ and this is seen through missing the small, subtle signals that the body gives to attempt to hold our hands and guide us throughout a world which is confusing. And it is in losing this physical, spiritual, and psychological irrigation with that which guides us like a daimon – that we risk losing ourselves.

The modern Roman hand has reignited the Olympic torch and carries it through the world to awaken the human body from a two millennia slumber where it was recognized as either a holding cell for the ‘ethereal’ or ‘spiritual’ world or as a piece of equipment for the purpose of labor. And today with the revitalization of physical culture its dignity and beauty returns, it is recognized not as sinful, nor debased and wicked, but as beautiful. The sport of pankration should be fought in open stadiums, amphitheaters, in open spaces with fresh air – in places where natural light can fall onto the body and create those moments that photography can capture like Michelangelo did for those he cast in marble: the clinch, long and tiring wrestling, the thai plum, blood, sweat, the facial muscles contorting from exhaustion, the sensory and emotional and spiritual experience captured in this event with the conscious understanding that this display which dances on a tightrope between the primitive and the great man is the soil from which we were all born, the soil from which the heights of the achievements of civilization – the finest works of visual art, the greatest works of literature, the discoveries of modern science, the advancement of modern technology – were all able to emerge. The suppression and sacrifice of certain base impulses and drives, the elevation of nobler ones which are needed to create something which stands beyond the moment and extends into eternity – all of this emerges from the literal, visceral instinctual energy that is expressed throughout this exchange.

This sport ought to not be witnessed as fleeting and momentary scenes of mere entertainment, but viewed as art, high art, as an achievement in and of itself for all of mankind: these moments of athletic greatness are evidence that man is still capable of inflicting the most primitive and raw violence while, at the exact same time, possessing the most noble traits which have been cultivated purposefully throughout the process of civilization which these athletes display on their surface and in their depths: discipline, focus, the ability to maintain calm under pressure, the ability to think beyond the present moment, the capacity for sacrifice, and courage – the ability to feel extreme fear and press on. They possess all of these traits and like statues they stand as living testaments to those around them – the athlete stands on a tightrope between an animal and a Greek god.

The emergence of religion as a biological and historical event

The transition from animal to man was not an easy or small task: the highest task and the greatest accomplishment of humanity as a whole is the capacity for self control of even the strongest impulses, and to instill something this important and unnatural into an animal it had to be done deliberately, repeatedly, and cruelly. How were these rules shared before the advent of reading or writing, how were the rules for conduct shared in the days where there was no advanced verbal language? It was through violence and punishment, and in this sense entire religious works had been impressed upon the human body, one whip at a time, one beating at a time, one whimper at a time prior to the existence of language, causing language to be created to write down these very laws. And what are religious works? They are books of strict and exacting rules and regulations for behavior intended to control human beings in an extremely precise, exacting manner so that a person has to follow this and this law and not take this action or have this thought so that they will become something completely different. A million possibilities, only one outcome that is remarkably similar to others, successful due in part to the careful and deliberate grooming of this person to mimic an idealized, noble, or “heavenly” person such as the Roman warrior or the Christian saint.

Violence chained impulse, it made impulse restrained, second guessed, it forced a pause and in this pause, with the advent of language which occurred as a result, there became a thought – should I? is it right? what are the consequences? translated into will I be punished, banished, or exiled for doing this? This pause is the genesis of our conscious existence.

It is possible that evolution and the development of consciousness itself is nothing more than the trauma passed down from generation to generation which has been genetically inherited from the blood of early humans whipped and burned and tortured by other humans and now this has become our blood and our bodies. There is no need for a cage or whip or brand, consciousness has already ensnared and trapped human beings with guilt, shame, and remorse in what now amounts to a biological prison: no remaining police, no other prisoners, only a whip and a mirror and a capirote. Religious law and criminal law were indistinguishable, religion in its attempt to create a higher type of person necessitated tightened restrictions, tightened punishments, and tightened discipline to create people like diamonds from pressure. And like the blobfish who is accustomed to living under extreme pressure at extreme depths, the human being who has now been exposed to the ridiculously liberated and unrestricted and relaxed morality generated by free market capitalism soon becomes uglier and more repulsive in its moral and aesthetic vacuum.

Capitalism is antithetical to the entire course of human evolution in that it rewards those who are the most disinhibited, the most immoral, the most deregulated. The Christian saint and martyr, the Roman athlete and warrior, both of these types created and embodied the spirituality and physicality of their respective societies. And here in its absentia, an issue appears in regards to the shift from the Romans to the Christians to capitalism: what type of person does capitalism attempt to create? What type of person embodies the values of free market capitalism, and does this person lead to an improvement from the state of mere humanity, or does it lead to a further decline into mere life.

Free market, unrestricted capitalism is the return to the primitive state of human nature that existed prior to religion and civilization, the true and disinhibited state of nature, where in lacking a gardener the garden soon becomes filled with weeds and vines which choke the flowers to death.
